Model Outline

1 Goal

We build a stack of models that simulate the process of preference formation and an election in the electorate of the population of Aarhus. Once for the municipal election and once for the national election.

First, we will focus on the municipal election.

2 Data-driven agent-based modeling

The modeling process is data-driven (Lorenz 2021). That means, empirical data is used for exploratory data analysis and initialization, calibration, and validation of the model. Details about the data-driven modeling process can be found in the data-driven ABM part.

3 Models

As specifiec in the proposal and the Specification Document D04.1, we have a stack of 6 models that are connected to each other as shown in the Figure 1. Here we specify this chain of models further with the goal to implent the first prototype. To that end, we also give an overview of the data sources used for initialization and validation.

We focus on the following models in the first prototype in the following form:

Model 2: Preference Formation (how agents form their preferences ) Model 3: Candidates and Parties (set up exogenously from data)
Model 4: Election Setup (set up as the orignal voting system in Aarhus and then alternative voting systems)
Model 5: Voting
Model 6: Election (aggregation of votes into results)

4 Data Sources

These are the data sources we use for initialization and validation of the models. Additionally, some data sources are also used as input data in model execution.

  • Census: Census data for the population of Aarhus
  • Survey:Survey responses about beliefs, preference on national and local issues, and voting behavior in the last election.
  • Candidates:Kandidattest answers for all participating candidate in the last municipal election in Aarhus.
  • Corpus: A corpus of local news articles from the last year in Aarhus tagged for the local topics in the survey.
  • Election results: Results from the last municipal election in Aarhus. Results from the last national election in Aarhus.

Flow:

  1. Model 3 and Model 4 are set at the beginning. The options are the lists in candidates of the political parties. We do not model the process of list generation, but we take it as given and take it from data. The election setup is making ballots from the list of candidates this can vary when we test alternative voting systems.

  2. We use census data (1) and the survey data (2) to initialize the agents in Model 2. We provide different ways how survey data goes into the initialization. The goal is to not use it excessively and maybe more in the form of extracted mental models using long term party preferences. The idea would be to have the distribution of preferences emerging from belief and preference formation (Model 2).

  3. Model 2 is then run for a number of time steps. The agents may perceive news articles (4) as stimuli and update their beliefs and preferences. The also share their beliefs and preferences with other agents. The survey data (2) is used to validate the belief and preference formation model.

  4. Model 5 is a one shot voting model which takes the preferences of the agents and the election setup and in particular the Kandidattest answers of candidates as input and produces the votes (or abstentions) for all agents based on their preferences. So, in the current voting systems the model produces for each voter either abstention of a candidate of a party to be put on the ballot. The survey data (2) together with the Kandidattest answers are used to validate the voting model. They can also be used as the input data, while in a full counter factual scenario study we would not use them but rather use the output of the belief and preference formation model.

  5. Model 6 is the aggregation of votes into election results. The election results of simulated elections are then validated against the real election results (5 and 6).
